Sometimes, showing up makes a difference.

You don't need perfect words or all the answers to support someone. Listening, being present, and helping them connect with support can matter more than you realize.

Learn How to Help

Five ways to support someone

1. Reach out

Check in when someone seems withdrawn, overwhelmed, or unlike themselves. A simple "How have you really been feeling lately?" can open a conversation.

2. Listen without judging

Let them speak without rushing to fix everything. Take their feelings seriously instead of telling them they should simply be grateful or stay positive.

3. Ask directly when concerned

If you are worried about suicide, ask calmly and directly whether they are thinking about suicide. Take their answer seriously.

4. Help them find support

Offer to help them contact a trusted adult, counselor, healthcare professional, or crisis service. You do not have to manage the situation alone.

5. Keep checking in

A thoughtful message or another conversation later can remind someone that they have not been forgotten.

These suggestions reflect guidance from the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline .

When someone may need immediate help

Talking about suicide, expressing hopelessness, feeling trapped, or withdrawing from others can be warning signs. Take concerning changes seriously.

If someone may be in danger, stay with them if it is safe to do so, involve a trusted person, and connect them with appropriate professional help. Do not promise to keep a suicide plan secret.

In the United States, call or text 988 for crisis guidance. If there is immediate danger, call 911.

You don't have to be someone's therapist to be there for them.

You can listen, care, encourage them to seek help, and remember to look after your own wellbeing, too.
